# Episode 1 - Between Stone and Sky

**Narrator:** 
Hello listener, I’m The Narrator and welcome to Tales Beneath the Surface, a serialised journey into the unknown. Try to get comfortable… good luck with that.

But what is a city? Many have asked, but few truly know. Some would say it’s just the buildings. Those towering edifices clipping the sky, man's dreams clawing the clouds. Or perhaps it’s the people? All those souls. Unwashed humanity scratching out an existence, mired in the mundane. Beady eyes and messy minds, just… being. 

[ *uncomfortable shiver* ]

I think we have all walked through the city at night and felt a little…off? A little, strange, uneasy? Perhaps it is a pang of fear, a primordial misfiring, the streets can look different in the dark after all, all those ever trudging passengers more… sinister.

I think a city is about what is hidden just beneath the surface, out of sight, waiting to be discovered.

## Absorbed

Pubs spit out flushed faces: waves of exaggerated smiles and frowns spilling laughter into the cold. Beer jackets fade quickly, no match for London’s sharp November. The kind of night that bites and rattles your bones but I’m wrapped up. Hoodie thick, gloves worn down by living, hands warm from the heat of creation.

Shoreditch cobbles stretch ahead, ankle breakers for the pissheads and party girls stumbling home. Not for me. I cut through the streets dodging the usual stares. They don't see me, not really—just another hooded figure lost in the dark. Fine by me.
I keep moving, my head low. This place is different now: alive and wild. Too many people for my work. I feel the wind cut my face, bitter and biting, nose running, this cold won't shift. Every step takes me deeper into the maze. I’m not here to mingle. No drinks, no chats. Just me and my tins. I like it this way. 

Tins, caps and pens are packed tight in my backpack, clinking like promises waiting to be spilled. There's always a wall waiting. Something that’s not quite finished. My job’s to fix it, complete the piece, make it say something.

I’m not like those who just cover. No, I respect the work, but I do make it mine. Add, never erase. Make my mark.

Tonight might be different. There's something like… hunger. Like Hackney has a growling stomach, like the streets want more than just paint. I shake it off, paranoia creeping in from too many sleepless nights and that lapsed prescription I’ll fill tomorrow, yeah, tomorrow…
I see it—a stretch of wall under dim light. Half-covered in symbols I don’t recognise. Definitely not someone local, I don't know the style. Probably for the best.  I don't want to get aggro from some lad for fucking with his piece. It is well abstract though, twisted in ways that shouldn’t make sense but somehow do. Yes, mate! I can work with this. It’s calling to me. I can finish this. I will finish this.

I step closer, my fingers brush the rough brick. I pause for a moment, thinking about what’s to come. There’s a weight about this, yeah, this is the one. Something needs to be released.  I shiver then shake it off. Just the cold. Just the night fucking with me. I grab my first tin, the hiss a familiar comfort and start to work. 

***

The first connection is simple. My hand glides over the existing lines, adding strokes and shaping forms. Symbols I’ve never seen but somehow know exactly where to place. Paint flows, my wrist moves easily, muscle memory. I’m not just tagging any more; I’m creating fresh narrative, something real.

It’s called “flow state,” at least that’s what some bloke said on a YouTube video I half-watched between the ads for supplements. The guy rambled on about how it’s like jujitsu or some shit. It wrestles with your subconscious until you submit. It’s supposed to be this “in the zone” thing where time bends and reality burns, where every stroke feels like a chat with the universe. 

Whatever it is, I’m there now. Outside fades, the alley falls silent, and it’s just me, my tins, and the fates. The chaos evaporates; no, I’m not just tagging—I'm making a deeper connection… right? Man, I’m hungry. 

The symbols pull me in deeper with every line. Paint linking like veins, connecting capillaries. My adrenaline spikes, sweat cold on my brow. I’m being watched. No, not watched. Tasted. No, that don’t make sense. Right?

My chest tightens. Breathing feels too slow, too shallow. I shake my head, try to focus, but the wall’s breathing too. The bricks seem closer now. I need to get that prescription filled. 

I tell myself it’s nothing, just my head playing tricks. No mask and too much ethylene. My heart floods my ears, drowning out the hiss. My hands shake, the tin slipping for a second. I freeze.

This is fucking stupid. It's just paint, just a wall. But my gut’s twisting, knotting tight, a belly full of broken glass. Something’s off, Something’s not right but I’m too deep in to stop. Can’t stop now.

I step back, breath misting in the air. The wall shifts—god I’m so tired. The alley’s empty. No one around. Churn, churn, grumble, grumble. My guts know something I don’t.

I’m hungry. Am I hungry? Hard to tell, I ignore the sensation. I’m too far in now. Can’t stop. Focus on my work and look longingly at my creation on the brick as the smiling mouth begins to emerge from the symbols. It fits better than it should, settling into the cracks like it’s always belonged. I can almost taste the paint on my tongue, acrid and metallic. 

***

The second link. My hands move faster, almost frantic now, but it feels so right. The markings are merging, making sense, talking to a part of my brain that’s limbic, a warning I'm ignoring. There’s a weight to it, like this piece matters more than anything. Each stroke feels like a secret, a rhythm I didn’t know existed. My heart races, thudding, all drum and no bass. 

And the bricks—they’re shifting, moving under my hands, like the wall itself is breathing. Is it? It's pulse sync with mine. I pause, look around. Nothing. Just cold air and tungsten lights. Focus. Breathe. I’m almost done. I can’t stop now.

My mind buzzes, energy coursing through me, urging me on. I spray my last. I step back, leaning on the wall for support, and look up, admiring my work. My eyes dampen. It’s good. No—it’s perfect.

And then I feel it. My hand... sinking. 

***

The wall shifts under me, soft, wet. My fingers sink deeper, but I barely notice, too caught up in the lines, the flow. My focus is locked on the shapes. When I brush something pitted and slick.

I glance down. My hand halfway into the wall. It’s not brick. It feels warm, alive. Panic flickers, but it’s distant, muffled by the strange calm that’s settled over me. I half-heartedly try to pull back, but my arms are heavy, slow. Part of me doesn’t even want to. Between the panic and the strange calm settling in, I’m not sure if I should fight it or just let it happen. It’s almost... blissful. 

I feel it tighten, something sharp pressing, and then—snap. My arm breaks. I hear it, feel it, somewhere far off, but the pain's distant, the sensation blurs, losing shape, becoming unreal. The wall’s biting down, pulling harder, but I just stand there, numb. It’s like the pain’s not mine, just another sound in the city’s voice.

A tremor of fear courses through me and I smell my own piss. The wall is breathing, It needs me for its nourishment. My hand, my arm... being chewed by the brick. But I can’t stop staring.

There’s a soft hum now, vibrating through the air, through me. The symbols... something about those symbols. They were something else. I feel it now, the weight of it, the power in the marks. My marks? 

The brick grinds deeper—crunch,  bone bending, breaking under pressure. My body splitting apart. Ribs splayed, my steaming offal on display. The wall pulls me, each second stretching into eternity. I can’t scream; the air has thickened, pressing against what's left of my lungs. A wet slurp echoes as it swallows more of me, the taste of damp earth and old stone wrapping around my senses. There’s a flicker of fear, but that candle is snuffed, smothered by the silence inside. This is my end.

Maybe I could run now? That seems a foolish thought. I can’t. I don't want to. I’m too focused, too deep in the flow. The paint was never just paint. It’s part of the wall now. I'm part of it now. 

I am the third link, the final component to this beautiful working. 

My legs buckle, my shins snap, and the brick engulfs me. It’s satisfied, finally sated. The last thing I see, no feel as I slide down its gullet, is the curl of its smile— grinning teeth, sharp and long. My lips tear, my jaw breaks - I smile back.

**Narrator:**
Goodness, what an unfortunate end. That poor, poor man. I mean, eaten by a wall! Consumed by the city itself, weaved into its fabric, the world after all, can be a cruel and confusing place can’t it?

But when the world wants something from you, it doesn't knock, it just barges in. Uninvited, unwelcome, but utterly certain that it belongs. You might struggle, momentarily, to recall the second this interloper crossed the threshold. 

Only noticing when your life’s worth has been turned over, ransacked. And what's lost? Time, clarity, dignity, or something deeper, something shaped like fate? Well then you have to pull your socks up, adjust your posture and just shamble on. Don't let the bastards grind you down, after all.
